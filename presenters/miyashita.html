Mizuki Miyashita

I am a professor of linguistics at the University of Montana. I was born and raised in Yamagata, Japan. After graduating from high school in Yamagata and completing a year of academic English training in Tokyo, I moved to the United States to receive higher education. I received my B.A., M.A., and Ph.D. in linguistics from the University of Arizona; my dissertation was on phonology in Tohono O’odham, an Indigenous language spoken in southwestern Arizona, US, and northern Sonora, Mexico. I joined the University of Montana as a linguistics professor in 2003. My specializations are documentary linguistics and phonetics and phonology. The current focus of my research is documentary linguistics in Blackfoot, which began with a collaboration with the late Mr. Darrell Kipp at the Piegan Institute in Browning, Montana. I have recorded lullabies and other songs, narratives, conversations, and isolated words in Blackfoot. I am currently engaged in research and outreach funded by the National Science Foundation’s Dynamic Language Infrastructure Program (NSF-DLI). My research focuses on documentation and application of rhythm and melody in Blackfoot. I am also engaged in Community-Based Research; I established the Collaborative Language Planning Project (CLPP). In the summer of 2022, I co-directed the Institute on Collaborative Language Research 2022 which was held on the UM campus.

Tools for learning Blackfoot word melody: Pitch Art, Praat and MeTILDAy

1. Rhythm and Melody in Blackfoot (Mizuki Miyashita) It is challenging for English speaking learners to perceive the rhythm and melody of Blackfoot words. To address this, my team has been developing a tool to help learners perceive the unique rhythm and melody of Blackfoot called MeTILDA. This tool allows users to extract the rhythmic and melodic elements from actual words and play them back in isolation (without vowel or consonant sounds). This tool can be utilized by teachers when instructing pronunciation. I will describe the unique rhythm and melody of Blackfoot and how second language learners may make pronunciation errors with respect to rhythm and melody. Then I will explain how MeTILDA works and potential impact on pronunciation teaching. 2. How to use Pitch Art in pronunciation instruction (Naatosi Fish) Pitch Art is a visual teaching tool that represents the word melody of Blackfoot words. By illustrating how pitch rises and falls across syllables, it helps learners sound more native-like. This workshop introduces the use of Pitch Art with learners of Blackfoot to support pronunciation. Participants will receive a brief overview of the online tool used to create Pitch Art, an introduction to the concept of word melody, and strategies for using Pitch Art in the classroom. 3. Praat and MeTILDA workshop (Mizuki and Naatosi) This workshop is offered for participants interested in using MeTILDA to create Pitch Art images for Blackfoot Word Melody. Participants will receive a brief introduction to understanding word melody, followed by have hands-on training in MeTILDA to create pitch art
